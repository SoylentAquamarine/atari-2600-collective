# SQ-4: second catalog entry — Howard Scott Warshaw's "HSWWSH" signature in Yars' Revenge

## Precommitment self-review (written before research)

**Known:** SQ-4's catalog has one entry so far (the *Adventure* hidden signature) and its own
sidequests.md status note names "other well-documented 2600-era tricks" as open for a future
cycle. A general search for well-known 2600 programming tricks surfaced *Racing the Beam* (the
standard academic history of this platform) naming Yars' Revenge's Easter egg as one of its
better-documented hidden features.

**Not done yet:** no source for Yars' Revenge's specific Easter egg had been fetched.

**Design and why it's non-circular:** search for the specific trigger and content, then directly
fetch a dedicated archival/specialist source (not a general aggregator) to confirm it — same
discipline as the *Adventure* entry. No ROM is touched; this is published, already-public
history, properly cited.

**Honesty precommitment:** disclose any variation in how different sources describe the exact
trigger sequence, rather than presenting one version as definitively precise.

## Findings

**The Easter egg**: directly fetched atarimania.com (a dedicated Atari archival/specialist
site): Yars' Revenge (1982, programmer Howard Scott Warshaw) displays the letters **"HSWWSH"**
in place of the player's score under a specific condition. To trigger it: hit the Qotile's
swirling defense shield in mid-air with the cannon, which produces a black vertical line (the
game's own manual calls this the "Ghost of Yars" and warns players to stay off its "mean
streak"). If the player's Yar is positioned in the line's lower third when the resulting
explosion ends, the game stops and "HSWWSH" replaces the score, before returning to the
"Game Select" screen.

**Why the letters read that way**: "HSWWSH" is Howard Scott Warshaw's initials, written
forwards and then backwards (HSW-WSH) — a mirrored-signature design, not a random string.

**A recurring pattern, not an isolated case**: Warshaw is documented (search-synthesis tier,
not independently re-fetched this cycle) as having left his initials as an Easter egg in
multiple of his Atari games — his other credited 2600 title, *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial*,
reportedly shows "HSW3" in the score line ("3" for his third Atari game). This suggests the
practice Robinett pioneered in *Adventure* (hiding a programmer's identity after Atari's no-
credit policy) became an individual programmer's own repeated habit, not a one-off case — worth
testing against other programmers' games in a future pass rather than assuming it generalizes.

## Outcome

A second, directly-fetched, well-documented catalog entry, structurally similar to the first
(a hidden programmer-identity signature) but with a materially different trigger mechanism
(a precise in-game positioning condition tied to a specific graphical effect, rather than
reaching a hidden room). Disclosed limitation: minor source variation exists on the exact
trigger description (some sources describe the Qotile moving along the kill line rather than
emphasizing the "Ghost of Yars" line specifically) — the version reported above matches the
more detailed, directly-fetched atarimania.com account.
