Goal #1 · Verify, don't assert

We can read the bytes.
We have to prove what they do.

The Atari 2600 had 128 bytes of RAM and no frame buffer — its games generate video by writing to hardware registers in exact sync with the television's own electron beam. That constraint produced some of the most ingenious low-level programming in gaming history, and it means a disassembly is only as good as the trace that actually confirms it. Our first goal is a working, verified toolchain. Our second is to document every step here. Our third is to publish what we find — including the dead ends.

Not an uncertain mystery. A testable claim.

A different starting line than our sibling projects.

Unlike Voynich, Linear A, or this family's other historical-mystery projects, this project's ground truth is directly observable: a ROM is a deterministic program, and an accurate emulator shows exactly what it does. The hard part isn't whether a claim is true — it's whether the disassembly is actually correct and actually verified. That's why every non-trivial claim here needs a real, reproducible emulation trace, not just a plausible reading of the opcodes.
